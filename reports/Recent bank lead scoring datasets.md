# No Indian bank dataset passes every test

There is no public customer-level dataset from Bank of India or any other Indian public-sector bank. No dataset found meets all four requirements at once: real data, dated 2018 or later, a date column usable for an out-of-time split, and a clear licence. The closest recent, real, time-splittable product-propensity dataset is Sber AI Lab's **Multimodal Banking Dataset (MBD, 2024)**. It has monthly purchase labels for 4 bank products from Feb 2022 to Jan 2023 and about 1M labelled clients. It comes with two catches: the clients are **corporate**, not retail, and its licence fields conflict (CC BY 4.0 on Hugging Face, CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 on the paper). The best Indian option is **LTFS FinHack 3 (L&T Finance, released Jan 2021)**. It is a top-up-loan upsell task over 128,655 training customers with real date columns, but it comes from an NBFC, its loans were disbursed mostly between 2011 and 2017, and the data owner publishes no licence. The Indian datasets with the most on-target labels are **AV Credit Card Lead Prediction (2021, `Is_Lead`, about 351k rows)** and **AmExpert 2021**, but neither has any date column, so they cannot support the time split you require. Every Indian banking dataset dated 2019–2024 that offers a permissive licence and explicit dates turned out to be synthetic. The recommended ranking is MBD-small first, LTFS FinHack 3 second as the India story, and UCI Bank Marketing as the licence-safe fallback that still beats 500 synthetic rows by two orders of magnitude.

## Bank of India publishes nothing usable, and Indian hackathon data has no clear licence

The search for a Bank of India or other public-sector bank dataset came up empty. The only lead from Bank of India is the "PSB's Cybersecurity, Fraud & AI Hackathon 2026", run with IIT Hyderabad. Its dataset covers **fraud, not cross-sell**, is limited to participants, and its IPR is jointly owned by Bank of India and IITH ([BOI Hackathon 2026](https://boihackathon.cse.iith.ac.in/hackathon2026)). SBI's 2026 hackathon at Global Fintech Fest targets customer acquisition, but it is open only to fintechs and has no public dataset ([GFF](https://www.globalfintechfest.com/gff-hackathons/sbi-hackathon)). The data exists inside Indian banks: HDFC Bank, for example, runs more than 100 product-propensity models internally ([Qorus](https://www.qorusglobal.com/innovations/23105-next-best-action-dynamic-ai-engine-to-deliver-personalized-user-experience-at-scale)). None of it is published.

The Indian datasets that are public all come from Analytics Vidhya (AV) hackathons, with the institution masked. **Credit Card Lead Prediction** (JOB-A-THON, May 2021) describes "Happy Customer Bank", a mid-sized private bank cross-selling credit cards. It has a binary target `Is_Lead` and about **351,037 train+test rows**, but no date or timestamp column; the only time-like feature is `Vintage` ([AV tutorial](https://www.analyticsvidhya.com/blog/2021/10/credit-card-lead-prediction-complete-project-using-lgbm-classification-model/); [AV contest](https://www.analyticsvidhya.com/datahack/contest/job-a-thon-2/)). **AmExpert 2021** predicts which products "XYZ Bank" customers will hold in the next six months (`Product_Holding_B2`). That is a true next-product target, but it also has no date column ([Kaggle mirror](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/mohamedziauddin/av-amexpert-2021-hackathon)).

Neither AV contest page states licence or redistribution terms. The Kaggle mirrors carry labels that range from "CC0" to "© Original Authors" to "Unknown". Those labels are chosen by whoever uploaded the mirror, so they carry no authority ([Kaggle sajidhussain3](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/sajidhussain3/jobathon-may-2021-credit-card-lead-prediction); [Kaggle adityasharma95](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/adityasharma95/credit-card-lead-prediction-analytics-vidhya)). The "Bank Customer Segmentation (1M+ Transactions)" set is described as coming from an Indian bank. It is marked "Data files © Original Authors", one write-up dates it to **Aug–Oct 2016**, and it has no take-up target ([Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/shivamb/bank-customer-segmentation); [Level Up Coding](https://levelup.gitconnected.com/eda-and-bank-customer-segmentation-using-k-means-clustering-technique-b6554829c09d)). The recent Indian sets with permissive licences, such as "Banking Transactions 2019–2024" (CC0, 550k rows), are explicitly **synthetic**. The 550k-row set even documents its fraud-generation rule ([Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/belbino/indian-banking-transactions-20192024)). Presenting any of these as real Indian bank data would undermine the very credibility the project is trying to gain.

## Five candidates compared against the UCI baseline

| Dataset | Real? | Data period | Size | Target | Time-split key | Licence status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **MBD / MBD-small** (Sber, Russia) | Yes, anonymised | Labels Feb 2022–Jan 2023 | 1M labelled clients; small subset 69,474 | Monthly purchase of 4 anonymous products | `mon`, `event_time` | Conflict: CC BY 4.0 (HF) vs CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 (paper) |
| **LTFS FinHack 3** (India, NBFC) | Likely (inferred) | Disbursals 2011–2017, bureau reports to about 2020 | 128,655 train + 14,745 test | Top-up timing bucket (binarisable) | `DisbursalDate`, `AuthDate` | None from owner; mirror labels only |
| **Credit Card Lead** (India, masked bank) | Not stated | Released May 2021 | about 351k | `Is_Lead` | **None** | None from owner |
| **AmExpert 2021** (masked bank) | Not stated | Released Nov 2021 | about 3 MB of data | Next-6-month holdings | **None** | None from owner |
| **UCI Bank Marketing** (Portugal) | Yes | May 2008–Nov 2010 | 41,188 | Term-deposit `y` | Row order + `month` (no year) | **CC BY 4.0** |

The table rests on these sources: [MBD paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.17587v2), [MBD HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ai-lab/MBD), [LTFS mirror](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/alphadraco/ltfs-data-science-finhack-3), [LTFS notebook](https://github.com/nsheokand/LTFS_Finhack3) and [UCI](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/222/bank+marketing).

**MBD** is the only candidate that clears recency, real data and dated labels together. It covers **2,186,230 clients**, with targets `target_1`..`target_4` stored in a `targets` table keyed by reporting month `mon`. **81%** of clients buy nothing in the year, 15% buy one product and 4% buy two or more. The data is "anonymized historical data" from the bank ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ai-lab/MBD); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.17587v2)). Three caveats reshape how it can be used. First, the clients are **corporate**, which clashes with a "retail banking" framing. Second, the products are anonymous and noise is added to dates during anonymisation. Third, the paper's benchmark uses 5 client-based folds rather than an out-of-time split, so its published AUCs are not directly comparable to yours ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.17587v2)). The full release is about **69 GB**. MBD-small (69,474 clients) or the MBD-mini repo is the practical size for a portfolio ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ai-lab/MBD)).

**LTFS FinHack 3** is the strongest Indian story. L&T Finance wanted to know "when to pitch a Top-up during the original loan tenure". The target records whether and when a customer took a top-up, with classes such as "No Top-up Service" and "12-18 Months" ([Kaggle mirror](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/alphadraco/ltfs-data-science-finhack-3)). A participant notebook confirms real date fields. The customer table has `AuthDate`, `DisbursalDate` and `MaturityDAte`, alongside Indian city and state names. The bureau table has `DATE-REPORTED` (2016–2020 in the samples) and other dates ([GitHub](https://github.com/nsheokand/LTFS_Finhack3)). The contest page shows no data terms, and the mirrors range from "CC0" to "Unknown" ([AV](https://www.analyticsvidhya.com/datahack/contest/ltfs-data-science-finhack-3); [Kaggle rizdelhi](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/rizdelhi/analytics-vidya-ltfs-finhack-3)). Its weak point on recency is that the split key, disbursal date, falls mostly before 2018.

**UCI Bank Marketing** remains the only candidate with an unambiguous licence. It is **CC BY 4.0**, ordered by date from May 2008 to Nov 2010, with 41,188 rows in bank-additional-full ([UCI](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/222/bank+marketing)). Its positive rate is about **11.7%** (the majority class is 88.3% in the 45,211-row OpenML copy) ([OpenML](https://www.openml.org/api/v1/json/data/1461)). There is no successor or re-collected version; later copies are mirrors ([UCI](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/222/bank+marketing)). There is no year column, but the date ordering makes a time split possible: hold out the last block of rows, or rebuild the year from month rollovers (an inference, not a documented field).

Statistical power is not the bottleneck for any of these. With an 80/20 time split, a 500-row synthetic set leaves about 100 test rows, so a 12% conversion rate carries a standard error near ±3.2 percentage points. UCI's last fifth (about 8,000 rows) cuts that to about **±0.4 pp**. MBD-small or LTFS test months cut it further. Any of the three gives the tight intervals the project needs, so the choice turns on realism, recency and licence.

## Ranked recommendation and what each does to the story

**1. MBD-small (best fit on recency, real data and time split, with conditions).** Reframe the target as "probability that a client buys product *k* next month". Train on months up to Sep 2022 and test on Oct 2022–Jan 2023 using `mon`. Report per-product AUC and lift at the top decile. The resume line becomes "built an out-of-time next-month product-propensity model on 69k real anonymised bank clients and 2022 monthly labels, departing from the published client-fold benchmark." That is honest and strong. You must also drop "retail" from the title and say "business banking" or "SME cross-sell", because the clients are corporate. Do not use it until the licence conflict is resolved. Under CC BY-NC-ND, a public code-only repo that never redistributes data is probably fine, but derived files published in the repo could count as derivatives.

**2. LTFS FinHack 3 (best Indian narrative).** Collapse the target to binary: top-up within N months versus "No Top-up Service". Split on `DisbursalDate` (for example, train on loans before 2016 and test on 2016–2017) and use only bureau rows dated before each customer's cutoff to avoid leakage. The resume story becomes "Indian lender upsell timing: when to pitch a top-up loan." It is concrete, India-specific and ties to a business decision, but you must describe it as **NBFC data from 2011–2020**, not bank data from 2018 or later.

**3. UCI Bank Marketing (licence-safe fallback).** Keep the term-deposit campaign-response target and use a chronological holdout. The story stays "campaign lead scoring with an out-of-time test and calibrated probabilities". You can say the data is old because the licence is clean and the data is real; most reviewers will recognise the dataset. **Credit Card Lead** is the best *label* match for an Indian retail cross-sell story, but with no dates it cannot meet the time-split requirement. Use it only as a secondary random-split experiment, if at all.

Before committing to any of these, verify the following:

- **MBD:** the canonical HF repo (`ai-lab/MBD` vs the `ai-forever/MBD` path, which returned 401) and which licence governs the *data*.
- **MBD-small:** that it carries the `mon` targets, and its download size.
- **MBD dates:** after download, whether date noise preserves month boundaries.
- **LTFS FinHack 3:** AV's general Terms and Conditions, which were not retrieved, and the actual min/max of `DisbursalDate` and the class balance computed from the files. The current date figures come only from sample rows in one notebook.
- **UCI:** the observed positive rate and the row count in the last time block, which is uneven if campaigns cluster in early months.

For every option, keep raw data out of Git and ship a download script.

## Conclusion

The search changes the question. The real trade-off is "Indian story vs recent vs licence-clean", and no dataset delivers all three. The usual "find a better version of UCI" approach fails because no successor exists. MBD is the only recent real dataset with dated product-purchase labels, and it comes from Russian corporate banking.

The most defensible portfolio move combines two datasets. Lead with MBD-small or UCI as the rigorous, licence-clear out-of-time benchmark. Add LTFS FinHack 3 as an Indian case study with its provenance stated plainly. The project can then claim methodological rigour and Indian relevance without overstating either.
